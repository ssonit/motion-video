# Night Studio - script (EN, 3:33)

Reworked from the reference: opens on the morning result, own wording, own role names, no quotes/benchmarks from the original.

**01 hook** - Picture this. You close your laptop at nine, and by eight the next morning a finished motion video is waiting for you. No all-nighter. Just five bots, each with one job.

**02 stall** - Most people who try this hit the same wall. The idea is fine, the render crawls, and the whole project quietly dies at ninety percent.

**03 missing** - What's missing isn't a smarter model. It's a studio. Someone to plan, someone to animate, someone to judge the result, and a queue to render it.

**04 crew** - So we hire five bots. The Director plans. The Animator writes the code. The Renderer makes the frames. The Critic scores them. The Courier delivers.

**05 nopicker** - One thing to know. You don't pick the model, and there's no effort dial. The bot chooses for each task. So your leverage is structure, not a magic prompt.

**06 laptop** - Second surprise. Your laptop can sleep. The work runs on a cloud computer that stays awake, shared by the whole crew.

**07 nogpu** - That machine has no graphics card. So skip heavy 3D engines and draw with HTML, CSS and SVG instead. Any processor handles those happily.

**08 folder** - Keep every project in one folder, and put the setup in a script. If the machine ever resets, run the script and you're back in a minute.

**09 skill** - Then write your rules once, as a shared skill. Every bot reads the same house style, so the video looks like one person made it.

**10 banned** - Add a banned list, too. No centered title on a gradient. No everything-fades-in-at-once. No confetti bursts. When a bad habit sneaks in, add one more line.

**11 states** - Bots forget and chats scroll away, but files don't. So each file marks a stage: brief, shot list, approved, code, final cut. One glance at the folder tells you where the project is.

**12 shotlist** - Before any code, the Animator drafts a shot list on a beat grid. You approve it, and only then does the real work begin.

**13 render** - Rendering isn't playback. Every frame is painted on its own from a single time value, so a slow frame just takes longer. It never gets dropped.

**14 blur** - Want smoother motion? Capture four moments per frame and blend them. It costs time on a weak machine, but it still finishes.

**15 beats** - Every visual change lands on a beat. The Director maps the rhythm of the music, and a click track lets you check it by ear.

**16 critic** - The Critic can't watch video, so it reads contact sheets, grids of key frames. It scores six things, and every one has to reach eight.

**17 loop** - Miss the mark and the video goes back for fixes. Three rounds at most. After that, it stops and asks you.

**18 night** - Put it together. At nine you approve the shot list. All night the Renderer checks its queue every hour. By morning the Courier drops the final cut in your lap.

**19 risks** - Know the weak spots. Your usage can run out. A long render can time out. A routine can get paused. Plan one fallback for each.

**20 outro** - That's the shift. Not one person, one chat, one video, but a small studio that works while you sleep. Start tonight with one bot and one shot list.
